## Why write about Net Energy Metering (NEM)?

Take a look at this bill from Southern California Edison.  Notice anything confusing?

<img src="images/nem_sce_electric_bill.jpg" width=700>

The amount due is $34.32, but the year-to-date charges are $212.29.  Which amount should you pay?  

Right on the invoice, it says *Understand Net Energy Metering (NEM) billing*.  Then, it explains that you are billed at the end of the year for the net difference between the amount of electricity that you purchased and the amount your solar panels generated and sold back to SCE.  

There is more to the story.  Notice that the bill is 8 pages long.   Scrolling down you will find another section.

<img src="images/nem_sce_page_5.jpg" width=700>

Page 5 details delivery charges.  The energy charge total is $155.80 for the current month and $56.49 for the previous month for a total year-to-date energy charge of $212.29.

<img src="images/nem_sce_page_6.jpg" width=700>

Page 6 details supply generation by Desert Community Energy.  The Additional Information section on the right states that the NEM balance owed is $98.72, but the Your New Charges are zero.

In simple terms, Desert Community Energy generates wind and solar energy and Southern California Edison charges a fee to deliver it.
The year-to-date total for energy generation is $98.72 and the year-to-date total to delivery the energy to your home is $212.29.

The amount due each month is for SCE maintenance and operation.  At the end of the fiscal year in May, the total of both generation and delivery will be offset by the total electricity produced by your home solar panels and sold back to SCE.
In order to plan for the yearly bill, you must keep track of the year-to-date totals and put money aside so you can pay it.
